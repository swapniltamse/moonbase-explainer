How the lunar network is specified

The rules that let many companies and agencies act as one network, the Moon's own GPS signal, the radio bands, and what keeps it all secure.

One network, many owners

LunaNet is the agreement that turns separate relays, ground stations and radios into one Moon-wide network, the way the internet joins networks that nobody owns outright.

Two ways to send data

Links to the Moon come and go as relays move and the Moon turns. So the network offers two kinds of delivery.

GPS for the Moon

Relays broadcast a navigation signal, the Augmented Forward Signal. Receive four or more from relays spread across the sky and you can solve for where you are, just as with GPS. The signal borrows openly from GPS design.

The Moon's navigation signal compared with GPS L1 C/A
Four links from “geometrically diverse relays” is what defines the navigation service. SRD 13
